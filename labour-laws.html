Labour Laws in the UAE

Understand the legal framework that governs employment in the UAE, your rights as an employee, and the responsibilities of your employer.

Introduction to UAE Labour Law

The UAE Labour Law regulates the relationship between employers and employees, covering a wide range of employment-related issues such as contracts, working hours, salary, leave entitlements, and termination. The UAE has a comprehensive set of regulations aimed at providing a fair and transparent working environment, while also supporting the country’s economic development goals.

Types of Employment Contracts

In the UAE, employment contracts are essential to define the relationship between employer and employee. The two main types of contracts are:

  • Fixed-Term Contracts: These contracts have a specified start and end date, typically ranging from 1 to 3 years. At the end of the term, the contract can be renewed, or the employee can be terminated according to the contract's terms.
  • Unlimited-Term Contracts: These contracts have no defined end date and automatically renew unless either party gives notice to terminate. The notice period typically ranges from 30 to 90 days, depending on the contract terms.

It's crucial for both parties to understand the terms laid out in their contracts to avoid misunderstandings or legal complications.

Working Hours and Overtime

The standard working hours in the UAE are 8 hours per day or 48 hours per week. These hours are usually spread over six working days, with Friday being the typical day off. However, working hours may vary for certain industries or specific roles.

Overtime work is allowed, but employers must compensate employees for overtime hours. Overtime is paid at a higher rate—usually 1.25 times the regular hourly wage for weekdays and 1.5 times the regular hourly wage for weekends and public holidays.

Salaries and the Wage Protection System (WPS)

The Wage Protection System (WPS) is an electronic salary payment system introduced by the UAE government to ensure that employees receive their salaries on time. Employers must pay salaries through the WPS, which helps monitor the timely payment of wages, prevent wage disputes, and ensure compliance with the labour laws.

Employees should receive their full salary as per the employment contract. If an employer fails to pay wages on time, employees have the right to file a complaint with the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE).

Leave Entitlements

UAE Labour Law entitles employees to various types of leave, including annual leave, sick leave, maternity/paternity leave, and public holidays:

  • Annual Leave: Employees are entitled to 30 days of paid annual leave after completing one year of service. For contracts shorter than one year, leave is calculated on a pro-rata basis.
  • Sick Leave: Employees are entitled to 90 days of sick leave per year, with pay. The first 15 days are fully paid, the next 30 days are paid at half salary, and any remaining days are unpaid.
  • Maternity Leave: Female employees are entitled to 45 days of maternity leave, with full pay, if they have been employed for at least one year. If the employee has worked for less than a year, she is entitled to unpaid leave.
  • Public Holidays: Employees are entitled to public holidays as declared by the government, such as National Day, Eid Al Fitr, Eid Al Adha, and others. If an employee is required to work on a public holiday, they are entitled to compensation or a day off in lieu.

Termination and End-of-Service Benefits

Termination of employment can occur for several reasons, including mutual agreement, resignation, or dismissal by the employer. In cases of unfair dismissal, the employee has the right to challenge the termination and seek compensation.

Employees are entitled to end-of-service benefits based on the length of their employment:

  • End-of-Service Gratuity: Employees who have worked for more than one year are entitled to an end-of-service gratuity payment. The gratuity is calculated as 21 days of salary for each year of service for the first five years, and 30 days for each additional year.
  • Notice Period: Employees must give a notice period when resigning, typically 30 days. Employers must also provide notice if they wish to terminate the contract.

It's important for both employers and employees to understand the terms of termination to avoid legal disputes and ensure fair treatment.

Health and Safety Regulations

The UAE has strict health and safety regulations to ensure that employees work in a safe environment. Employers are required to provide a safe workplace, including protective equipment and measures to prevent workplace accidents and injuries.

In the event of an injury or occupational disease, employees are entitled to compensation, including medical treatment and, in some cases, a one-time lump sum for long-term disability.

Dispute Resolution and Legal Rights

If a labour dispute arises between an employee and employer, either party can file a complaint with the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE). The Ministry will attempt to resolve the dispute through mediation. If mediation fails, the case can be taken to the Labour Court.

Employees have the right to seek legal recourse for unfair treatment, discrimination, or violation of rights under UAE Labour Law. It is recommended to keep all records related to employment, such as contracts, pay slips, and communications, to support any legal claims.
